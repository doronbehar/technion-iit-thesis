\chapter{Conclusion \& Outlook}

Our software \yotam{what do you mean by 'our software'} tool has enabled us to explore phenomena that were hard to anticipate, and hard to model. These include:

\begin{itemize}
	\item Choosing wisely laser cooling intrusion angles is significant.
	\item Rogue laser cooled ions can undermine sympathetic cooling.
	\item The conserving of angular momentum of sympathetically cooled ions, limits their cooling rate.
	\item The sympathetically cooled ions' conserved angular momentum, depends on their initial temperature, so we identified this as hysteresis.
	\item Increasing the trap's secular frequencies, helps slightly overcome the angular momentum conservation limit, by inducing more frequent collisions with the cooled crystallized laser cooled ions.
\end{itemize}

Additionally, Baba \& Waki's model for sympathetic cooling in quadrupole traps\cite{BabaWakiSympatheticCooling}, doesn't derive equations that could anticipate a hysteresis phenomenon. Their model, and other models we found\cite{Plasma2T1,Plasma2T2} deal only with temperatures, and hence cannot anticipate a phenomenon like angular momentum conservation.
